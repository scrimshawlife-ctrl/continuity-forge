# Cinematic Symbolism Corpus
# Dedicated corpus of filmic techniques used symbolically. No fixed meanings. Meaning arises from pattern, contrast, recurrence, context, transformation, and relation to dramatic problem.

## Core Techniques (track as patterns, not symbols)

**Shot Scale**
- Extreme close-up on object or body part → isolates, intensifies pressure, removes context.
- Close-up vs wide: intimacy vs exposure or power differential.
- Recurrence with scale change signals transformation or loss of control.

**Camera Distance, Height, Focal Length**
- Low angle without cliché: can enclose or dominate depending on prior pattern.
- High angle: surveillance, diminishment, or protective overview.
- Wide-angle lens distortion: psychological pressure or spatial unease.
- Telephoto compression: emotional distance or fatal convergence.

**Depth, Frame Position, Negative Space, Offscreen, Occlusion**
- Negative space as active character or missing element.
- Offscreen sound or action that structures the visible.
- Partial occlusion: withheld knowledge, power, or threat.
- Reflection/mirror: doubling without explanation.

**Symmetry / Broken Symmetry**
- Perfect symmetry: order, ritual, entrapment.
- Broken symmetry: disruption, choice, imbalance.
- Recurrence of broken symmetry across scenes = accumulating disorder or agency.

**Repeated Composition / Graphic Match / Match Cut**
- Same framing across time: continuity of pressure or ironic contrast.
- Graphic match: transformation or substitution.
- Match cut on action: seamless but mutated meaning.

**Screen Direction, Blocking, Camera Motion**
- Consistent screen direction: stable world or compulsion.
- Reversal: inversion or crossing threshold.
- Camera orbiting vs static: entrapment vs observation.
- Blocking that repeats with mutation (see symbolic_blocking in symbolic-dramaturgy.md).

**Focus, Light, Color Relations**
- Shallow focus: subjective priority or isolation.
- Rack focus: shift of attention or revelation without dialogue.
- Color temperature shift: emotional or ritual temperature change.
- Color as relational (one character's color contaminates another's space).

**Sound Bridges, Silence, Acousmatic Sound**
- Sound bridge: link across cuts that carries symbolic charge.
- Silence: absence that becomes presence (residue).
- Acousmatic (sound without visible source): mystery, haunting, offscreen power.
- Motif transfer: sound migrates from object to character to environment.

**Editing Patterns**
- Ellipsis: time compression that hides transformation.
- Repetition of cut rhythm: ritual or compulsion.
- Sudden rupture in established rhythm: crisis or revelation through form.

**Motif Transfer Across Elements**
- Object → gesture → architecture → sound → editing structure.
- Example: a recurring object migrates into camera movement, then into edit cadence, then into silence.

**Rules for Cinematic Symbolism**
- Never assign fixed meaning (e.g., "red always = danger").
- Meaning = variation against established pattern + dramatic consequence + transformation.
- Track in cinematic_encoding output.
- Prefer subtle, accumulative use over foregrounded technique.
- Mutation across recurrences is mandatory for symbolic charge.

**Examples of Pattern Use (without fixed meaning)**
- Repeated low wide shot of a doorway that gradually includes more of the figure or excludes it.
- Consistent left-to-right movement that reverses in the final scene.
- A sound that begins diegetic and becomes acousmatic, then disappears.
- Composition that starts symmetrical and ends with one element removed from center.

This corpus is drawn from analysis of films that use form as latent symbolic structure (e.g., works by Kubrick, Bresson, Tarkovsky, Denis, etc.) but generalized into operations rather than auteur signatures.
